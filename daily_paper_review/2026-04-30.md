# Daily Paper Review — 2026-04-30

**Theme**: Diffusion Models & Representation Learning (survey + theory) + Information Theory for LLMs

## 1. Task & Motivation

The central question across today's papers: **what information do generative models actually learn, and when does that information become useful?**

- **[2407.00783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00783)** — Tao Hu et al. survey the bidirectional relationship between diffusion models (DMs) and representation learning: (a) using pre-trained DM representations as frozen feature extractors for downstream recognition tasks, and (b) injecting SSL/representation advances back into DM training to improve generation quality.

- **[2105.14257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14257)** — Mittal et al. (ICML 2021, updated v4 2024) pioneer diffusion-based representation learning by reformulating denoising score matching so that the encoded latent code explicitly controls the level of detail preserved — a key difference from VAE/GAN where the latent → data mapping discards information not needed for reconstruction. They achieve SOTA on semi-supervised image classification with infinite-dimensional latent codes.

**Core motivation**: DMs are simultaneously generative models and self-supervised learners, since denoising requires no labels. Understanding *what* they encode and *when* those representations peak in quality is both theoretically interesting and practically important for transfer learning.

---

## 2. Challenge

**Challenge 1: The representation quality vs. noise level puzzle.**

Diffusion models learn to denoise at every noise scale. At low noise, representations encode fine-grained detail; at high noise, they dissolve into generic structure. But at *intermediate* noise levels, something remarkable happens: representation quality for downstream tasks **peaks**, then degrades on both sides. Why?

**[2502.05743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05743)** (Xiao Li et al., NeurIPS 2025) provides the most compelling answer so far: the **unimodal dynamics** emerge from an interplay between denoising strength and class confidence across noise scales. Crucially, the presence of this unimodal curve is itself a **generalization indicator** — it appears when the model generates novel images, but collapses into a monotonically decreasing curve when the model memorizes training data. This is a rare case of a structural property of learned representations directly reflecting generalization vs. memorization.

**Challenge 2: No principled CoT evaluation without annotated data.**

Existing CoT reasoning evaluation methods either require expensive human-annotated intermediate reasoning steps, or rely on outcome-based metrics that miss failure modes (false positives where reasoning looks right but logic is wrong). The information-theoretic paper addresses this by quantifying *information gain* at each reasoning step, requiring no annotated CoT data.

**Challenge 3: LLM representations distribute information across tokens, not concentrated in any "special" token.**

Previous work (e.g., probing studies) often focused on the last token or specific "meaningful token" positions. The LLM geometry paper ([2402.03471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03471)) finds information is *distributed* across all tokens, and that Lasso regression can select meaningful tokens that sometimes outperform attention weights — complicating the simple "last token = task answer" assumption.

## 3. Insight & Novelty

**Insight 1 (from 2502.05743): Unimodality as a generalization thermometer.**

The unimodal representation curve is not just a curiosity — it's a **diagnostic tool**. The paper proves theoretically (using low-dimensional structure of image data) that the peak emerges when the DM successfully captures the underlying data distribution. Empirically, the shape of this curve reliably distinguishes generalization from memorization, without needing a held-out test set. This is practically valuable: a model practitioner can use this to detect overfitting early.

**Insight 2 (from 2105.14257): Controllable level of detail via latent code dimensionality.**

Unlike VAEs where the latent space is fixed-dimension and compresses away detail, diffusion-based representations allow **manual control of detail level** by adjusting what the latent code needs to encode for denoising. This makes it naturally suited for fine-grained vs. coarse representation tasks — a flexibility VAE/GAN-based approaches lack.

**Insight 3 (from CoT paper): Information gain, not outcome, measures reasoning quality.**

The key variable is `I(step_n; answer | step_1,...,step_{n-1})` — the mutual information between each reasoning step and the final answer, conditioned on all prior steps. High information gain means the step is genuinely contributing new evidence toward the answer; low gain means the step is redundant or confused. This is a clean information-theoretic formalization of what "reasoning progress" actually means.

**Insight 4 (from LLM geometry paper): Power law between representation entropy and model size.**

Representation entropy scales as a power law with model size, which the authors connect to the broader scaling law phenomenon via conditional entropy theory. Additionally, they show that information gain of new tokens is theoretically connected to ridge regression — bridging the gap between information theory and linear algebraic analysis of transformer weights.

## 4. Potential Flaws & Limitations

**On unimodal dynamics (2502.05743):**
- Theoretical analysis relies on **low-dimensional structure of image data** — whether this extends to text, code, or multimodal data is an open question.
- The generalization/memorization diagnostic is shown for classification tasks; whether the unimodal curve shape can detect these properties in generative tasks (where it matters most) is less clear.
- The paper uses DDPM/DDIM-style models; whether modern rectified flow or consistency models exhibit the same dynamics is unknown.

**On diffusion-based representation learning (2105.14257):**
- The semi-supervised setting requires a sufficiently diverse labeled set for the infinite-dimensional code. In low-data regimes, the advantage may shrink.
- Computationally heavier than contrastive SSL methods (SimCLR, MoCo) for comparable representation quality — the denoising objective is inherently more expensive per gradient step.

**On CoT information-theoretic evaluation:**
- The information gain computation requires access to the model's internal activations at each step, which is not always available (API-only models).
- Assumes the reasoning steps are provided in order — doesn't directly handle cases where CoT reasoning backtracks or revises earlier conclusions.

**On LLM geometry (2402.03471):**
- The power law relationship is discovered via simulations — the theoretical derivation, while intuitive, relies on strong assumptions about the data distribution.
- Focus on autoregressive transformers; findings may not generalize to encoder-only models (BERT) or hybrid architectures.

## 5. Researcher's Radar (Yu's Angle)

**Highest relevance to Yu's interests** (diffusion models, representation learning, information-theoretic methods, trustworthy AI):

1. **The unimodal dynamics paper (2502.05743) is the standout** — it directly connects representation quality peaks to generalization vs. memorization via information-theoretic reasoning. For someone working on diffusion models and representation learning, this provides a *principled diagnostic tool* for evaluating whether a DM has truly learned generalizable representations. The NeurIPS 2025 acceptance adds credibility.

2. **The survey (2407.00783)** is useful as a reference taxonomy, but given its publication date (June 2024), it likely misses the most recent developments (DiT, Flow Matching, modern Rectified Flow advances). Not a priority to read in depth.

3. **The CoT information-theoretic paper** aligns well with Yu's interest in information-theoretic methods and trustworthy AI. The information gain formalization could extend to evaluating reasoning fidelity in generative models more broadly. However, the experimental validation is limited to arithmetic tasks (GSM8K, toy arithmetic) — more complex reasoning (math proofs, code generation) needs verification.

4. **The LLM geometry paper** provides a theoretical scaffolding (power law + conditional entropy → scaling laws) that Yu might find useful for framing representation analysis, but the empirical work is more exploratory than the CoT or diffusion papers.

5. **Diffusion-based representation learning (2105.14257)** is a foundational paper to know but the field has advanced significantly since 2021. The current frontier is more about how modern large-scale DMs (DiT, SD3, Flux) behave as representation learners, not the ICML 2021 proof-of-concept.

**Potential research directions for Yu:**
- Apply the **unimodal dynamics diagnostic** to diffusion-based representation learning at scale — does the curve reliably detect generalization in large pre-trained DMs used as frozen feature extractors?
- Extend the **information gain** framework to evaluate CoT reasoning quality in multi-step image generation or video synthesis (where intermediate steps have semantic meaning).
- Investigate whether the **level-of-detail control** in diffusion-based representations (2105.14257) can be used for controllable representation learning in downstream tasks.
