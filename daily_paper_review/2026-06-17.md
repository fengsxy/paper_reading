## 1. LESS: Mutual-Stability Sampling for dLLMs — 72.1% Step Reduction with No Accuracy Loss
**arXiv:** 2606.16908 | LLaDA/Dream-7B/8B | ACL 2026

**Core idea:** Fixed-step diffusion decoding wastes computation on tokens that are already stable while committing unstable ones too early. LESS treats token commitment as an **online stopping problem** — each position is unmasked only when:
1. Top-1 prediction confidence is high
2. Top-1 token persists across recent reverse steps (stability over time)
3. Predictive distribution is stable under top-K inter-step **Jensen-Shannon divergence**

**Why it matters for your research:** This is a principled, training-free improvement to dLLM decoding. The JS-divergence criterion is essentially an information-theoretic stability measure — you're tracking when the distribution stops changing, which relates to your info-theoretic interest in convergence. 72.1% step reduction means ~3× speedup in wall-clock time for the same quality.

**Key insight:** The "mutual stability" framing — waiting for both within-token (confidence) and across-time (persistence) signals — is cleaner than entropy-only approaches. Worth comparing to DynHD's trajectory deviation approach.

## 2. Reflective Masking: Multi-Turn Reasoning in Mask Diffusion Models
**arXiv:** 2606.16700 | 22 pages | cs.CL

**Core idea:** Existing MDMs don't support multi-turn masking — they do one full denoise and done. **Reflective Masking (RM)** enables iterative local revision: the model can revisit and refine previous outputs based on evolving context, without discarding correct partial answers. Lightweight post-training, no architecture change.

Two mechanisms:
- **Reflective Masking:** iteratively unmask → refine → remask (local edits, not full regenerate)
- **History Reference:** leverages intermediate denoising states during revision (parameter-free, like AR chain-of-thought but for MDM)

**Why it matters:** This directly competes with AR chain-of-thought for reasoning tasks. The key advantage of RM over AR: local edits instead of full sequential regeneration. Tasks include Sudoku, text generation, image editing — strong generality claim.

**Connection to your FoCore work:** FoCore also uses convergence signals to guide refinement. RM's multi-turn approach is complementary — both are about using denoising dynamics to guide generation, but RM focuses on iterative revision while FoCore focuses on token-level confidence.

## 3. Path Convergence in Diffusion Models — 1/√p Scale, Infinite MSE, Extrapolation to p=∞
**arXiv:** 2606.12161 | Werner Krauth (École Normale Supérieure) | cond-mat.stat-mech

**Core idea:** For diffusion paths interpolating between a target distribution (known only through N patterns) and a reference distribution: convergence of finite-pattern paths toward the p=∞ path scales as **1/√(p)**, but with **infinite mean square deviation** at any finite p. Despite infinite MSE, you can still extrapolate to the p=∞ distribution.

**Why it matters:** This is a fundamental statistical mechanics / info-theoretic result about diffusion model path convergence. The 1/√p scaling is the classic rate for i.i.d. sample-based estimation. The infinite MSE at finite p is striking — it means naive Monte Carlo averaging of paths is problematic, but clever extrapolation still recovers the true distribution.

**Relevance to your interests:** If you're thinking about information-theoretic limits of diffusion models (which your info-theoretic methods interest suggests), this paper gives a clean, provable result. The extrapolation strategy for density estimation could be relevant to representation learning.

## 4. Local Coverage Governs Memorization in Diffusion Models — KDE Theory, Sharp Local Transitions
**arXiv:** 2606.14390

**Core idea:** Memorization in diffusion models is **not global** — the same model can produce both memorized and novel samples. Key result: memorization is governed by **local data coverage** (density of training samples in a neighborhood). Via the kernel density estimation (KDE) connection, they derive a theoretical criterion predicting which points are memorized based on:
- Local training data density
- Dataset size
- High-dimensional geometry (sharp local transitions: sparse regions → memorized, dense regions → generalization)

**Why it matters:** This challenges the common framing of memorization as a model-wide property. The coexistence of memorized and novel samples within one model is important for understanding what diffusion models actually learn. The KDE connection is theoretically elegant.

**For your research:** This is a good example of using information-theoretic / statistical arguments (KDE, high-dimensional geometry) to explain deep learning phenomena. Also relevant to trustworthiness concerns in generative models.

## 5. Effectiveness-Fluency Trade-Off in LLM Conditioning — Principled Understanding
**arXiv:** 2606.12234

**Core idea:** Controlling LLM outputs (via prompting, conditioning, guidance) involves a fundamental trade-off between **effectiveness** (meeting the control objective) and **fluency** (natural language quality). This paper provides a principled analysis of this trade-off — understanding why current approaches (classifier guidance, PPL-based rejection, etc.) all fail at some point.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to representation learning and trustworthy AI. If you're studying how models represent and process conditioning information, understanding the effectiveness-fluency boundary is important. Also relevant if you're working on guidance methods.

## 6. Climate Model Emulation with Diffusion: ParamDiffusion vs. Deterministic Baselines
**arXiv:** 2606.14570

**Core idea:** Diffusion models for regional climate downscaling (precipitation). They introduce **ParamDiffusion** — a two-stage diffusion-based framework — and compare against deterministic ML baselines. Key question: **what's the added value of generative (diffusion) over deterministic methods** for scientific domain applications?

**Findings:** Comprehensive evaluation framework for climate science needs (including extreme precipitation events). Diffusion approaches offer an **uncertainty envelope** (ensemble of plausible fields) that deterministic methods cannot, which has genuine value for climate science decision-making.

**Why it matters:** This is a good applied diffusion paper showing where generative models genuinely add value beyond deterministic prediction. The two-stage (parametrization → diffusion) approach may inspire other scientific domain applications. Also validates that uncertainty quantification is a key advantage of diffusion-style approaches.
