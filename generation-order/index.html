Generation Order and Parallel Decoding in Masked Diffusion Models

An Information-Theoretic Perspective

Shaorong Zhang, Longxuan Yu, Rob Brekelmans, Luhan Tang, Salman Asif†, Greg Ver Steeg†

University of California, Riverside

† Equal advising

Abstract

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) significantly accelerate inference by trading off sequential determinism. However, the theoretical mechanisms governing generation order and the risks inherent in parallelization remain under-explored.

We provide a unified information-theoretic framework to decouple and analyze two fundamental sources of failure: order sensitivity and parallelization bias.

Key Insights

Our analysis yields three key insights:

  • Easy-First decoding — prioritizing low-entropy tokens — has benefits magnified as model error increases
  • Factorized parallel decoding introduces intrinsic sampling errors that can lead to arbitrarily large Reverse KL divergence, capturing "incoherence" failures that standard Forward KL metrics overlook
  • While verification can eliminate sampling error, it incurs an exponential cost governed by the total correlation within a block. Heuristics like remasking, though efficient, cannot guarantee distributional correctness
